Everything stays on your computer

Your music is never uploaded — there are no WavRead servers to upload it to. No account, no telemetry, no identifier. Four requests exist in the whole app: a daily version check you can switch off, an update you asked for, and the two model downloads you only trigger by using separation or lyrics.

How WavRead works

Drop in a finished track, a folder of stems, or capture a session straight from your DAW. WavRead measures tempo, key, loudness, dynamics, frequency balance, stereo image and structure on this computer, estimates the chords, melody and lyrics, and writes two documents: everything measured, and what it may mean.

Every number is labelled for what it is — measured, detected, estimated, or unavailable. WavRead never claims more certainty than the analysis allows.

Give it to any AI — optionally

AI assistants can't hear audio, but they can read. The Track Document contains everything an assistant needs to give deep, specific advice about your mix and arrangement — paste it into Claude, ChatGPT, or anything else. WavRead itself never requires AI: the built-in Feedback Report works completely offline.

Compare tracks

Put your mix next to a reference track — or next to last week's version of the same song — and WavRead reports the measured differences in plain language: loudness, dynamics, frequency balance, stereo width. Context decides whether a difference is good; WavRead just makes it visible.

Free during development

WavRead is free to use today, at version 1.4.4. It will become a paid app — a one-time purchase, not a subscription, since it runs on your computer and there is nothing for us to host.

That change is not tied to a version number. It happens when the app is code-signed and notarized, because a paid download should not warn you about an unidentified developer on first launch. Signing needs an Apple Developer account, which isn't in place yet. You'll be told before anything starts costing money.

Development status

1.4.4 — August 16, 2026

  • Capture a session straight from your DAW with the WavRead Bridge plugin (AU and VST3): the master, the parts, or both — armed together in one press
  • Capture the master alongside the parts and WavRead measures what the master bus adds: delay times, how long the reverb takes to decay, and the tone change through the bus
  • Every part is now measured for peak, true peak and clipping, so a track that arrived already clipped is named rather than hidden in the mix
  • How to use WavRead is now a page inside the app, and Update now installs a release after checking it against its published checksum

1.4.0 — August 15, 2026

  • Findings you can check: every finding that can be heard carries a Listen button that plays and loops exactly the range it stands on, and opens the graph it came from
  • A finding is now three separable parts — the measurement, what it may mean, and what to listen for. Processing recipes are gone: WavRead measures, your DAW edits
  • Stems are named as the source of a frequency band only when the data supports it, and never when the energy is spread across parts

1.3.0 — August 15, 2026

  • Measurement trust: peak was being measured on the mono fold-down, which hides a peak living in one channel by up to 6 dB — now measured on the real channels. Reanalyze in Settings to correct existing tracks
  • True peak (dBTP) by oversampling per ITU-R BS.1770, plus loudness range (LRA), peak-to-loudness, per-channel peaks, clipping count and DC offset
  • A delivery contract in every document, each value naming the standard it was measured to and the versions that produced it
  • Loudness points now sit at their real measurement positions rather than spread across the track
  • Values that could not be measured say so instead of showing a dash

1.2.1 — August 15, 2026

  • Mono switch: hear the mix summed to mono, so mono-compatibility findings can be listened to rather than only read
  • Skip ten seconds back and forward on the transport
  • Mix history: group mixes of one song as versions, note each, and see what the measurements did between them
  • Update checks that tell you a new version exists and never install anything
  • Fixed: “Hear the difference” did nothing until playback had started, and “Play this section” ran past the end of the section

1.2.0 — August 15, 2026

  • Listening markers: mark a moment while the track plays, write what you noticed, and see what the measurements say differs around it — loudness, activity, stereo width and correlation against the track and the section it falls in
  • Markers are written into the Track Document, so the copy, the downloads and the AI handoff carry your notes beside the measurements
  • Notes are stored outside the analysis, so reanalysis never destroys them

1.1.0 — August 14, 2026

  • Suggested EQ you can hear: an A/B during playback that compares your track flat against the suggested curve, level-matched so the comparison is not won by loudness. It previews the suggestion — nothing is written, exported, or altered, and playback always starts flat

  • First stable release; supersedes the 1.0.0-rc series
