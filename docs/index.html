Everything stays on your computer

WavRead analyzes audio on your machine. Your music is not uploaded or sent to WavRead servers — there are none. No accounts, no tracking, no telemetry.

One feature needs a download: lyric transcription fetches a speech model (about 460 MB) from its publisher the first time you use it, then caches it. That is the only network request WavRead makes, and it carries nothing about you or your music. Everything else — including melody — works with no connection at all.

How WavRead works

Drop in a finished track — or the separate stems of one song — and WavRead measures it: tempo, key, loudness (LUFS), dynamics, frequency balance, stereo image, and song structure. It separates the mix into drums, bass, vocals, and other instruments, estimates the chord progression, transcribes the lyrics, and writes the vocal melody as notes.

Everything lands in two documents:

Track Document — facts about this track. 
Tempo        ~79.5 BPM        measured
Key          G minor          estimated (confidence 0.86)
Loudness     -19.5 dBFS RMS   measured
Structure    8 sections — quiet intro, peak at 1:52, pull-back outro
Chords       Gm · A# · D# · Cm
Lyrics       transcribed with timestamps
Feedback Report — what the analysis suggests. 
What's working
• Strong dynamics: crest factor 17.6 dB.
• A clear hook recurs through the track.

What to fix or check
1. 45.9% of energy sits in the low mids (150–500 Hz) —
   the muddiness zone. Inspect the densest elements around 200–400 Hz.

Estimates are labeled as estimates, with confidence. Measurements are labeled as measured. WavRead does not pretend to be more certain than the analysis allows.

Give it to any AI — optionally

AI assistants can't hear audio, but they can read. The Track Document contains everything an assistant needs to give deep, specific advice about your mix and arrangement — paste it into Claude, ChatGPT, or anything else. WavRead itself never requires AI: the built-in Feedback Report works completely offline.

Compare tracks

Put your mix next to a reference track — or next to last week's version of the same song — and WavRead reports the measured differences in plain language: loudness, dynamics, frequency balance, stereo width. Context decides whether a difference is good; WavRead just makes it visible.

Free while in beta

WavRead is in open beta and free to use today. When it reaches 1.0 it becomes a paid app — a one-time purchase, not a subscription, since it runs on your computer and there is nothing for us to host. Beta users will be told before that happens.
